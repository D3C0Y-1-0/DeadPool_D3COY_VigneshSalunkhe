# 15 — Judge Q&A, Risk Register & Final Checklist

## 1. Tough questions and strong answers

### Concept
**Q1. How is this different from T-Pot or just running Cowrie with Kibana?**
Those give you honeypots and charts. They don't have an incident model. MIRAGE collapses events into one attack chain per attacker, scores and explains severity, correlates with real production telemetry, plants honeytokens in real systems, and offers human-approved response and intel export. It is a SOC workflow, not a data viewer.

**Q2. Honeypot hits are hostile by definition, so why do you need scoring?**
Confidence is high, but priority isn't equal: a drive-by banner grab and an intruder who stole data and pivoted need very different responses. We score progress, intent, and reach, which is what analysts need to prioritize.

**Q3. Can attackers detect honeypots and avoid them?**
Some can. We reduce that with realistic banners/filesystems and by deploying honeytokens on real systems, which a fingerprinting attacker can't avoid because they look like ordinary credentials/data. Avoiding the decoy is also a signal we can learn from (e.g., absence).

**Q4. Why rules and not ML?**
24 hours, tiny data, and analysts need to trust the score. Explainable factors beat opaque models here. The architecture supports learning-to-rank from analyst feedback as a roadmap item.

### Technical
**Q5. How do you map to ATT&CK?**
A data-driven YAML rule file maps normalized event patterns (action/outcome/command regex/flags) to technique IDs and kill-chain stages. It's version-aware: ATT&CK moved Defense Evasion into Stealth and Impair Defenses in v19, so tactic labels are config, not code.

**Q6. How do you correlate with real assets?**
By source identity across zones: all events (decoy and production firewall/auth) for the same IP within a window are pooled. Any production event for an attacker IP sets "real asset touched" and escalates. Internal benign traffic doesn't match, so no false positives. Honeytoken credentials add a second, identity-independent path.

**Q7. IP is a weak identity (NAT, proxies, Tor). How do you handle that?**
Acknowledged limitation. Mitigations: honeytokens (identity-independent), fingerprints (HASSH, SSH client, command patterns) for campaign clustering, and time windows. Roadmap: graph clustering and identity-provider correlation.

**Q8. What about scale?**
Demo scale is intentionally simple (re-evaluate per attacker). The design scales with a message queue (Redis Streams/Kafka), partitioning by source IP, and an incremental evaluator, plus ECS export to a real SIEM for long-term storage.

**Q9. Does it need internet?**
No. Whole stack is offline-capable. GeoIP/LLM/threat-feed enrichments are optional.

### Safety
**Q10. Can the honeypot be used to attack production or third parties?**
Not by design: decoys are on internal networks with no egress and no route to production or the SOC; containers are hardened; logs flow one way via read-only mounts; and we ship an isolation test. In real deployments, add VLAN/VPC egress denial.

**Q11. What if the response automation is abused (spoofed IP → block internal host)?**
Production-affecting actions need human approval, show a dry-run, use an allowlist of never-block ranges, and are audited.

**Q12. Is the "customer data" real?**
No. It's synthetic, generated by script with reserved `.example` domains. Bait credentials are fictitious and unique.

### Business / impact
**Q13. Who buys this?**
SOC teams and MSSPs that want high-fidelity detections with low analyst load; also compliance-heavy sectors (finance, healthcare) needing early breach detection.

**Q14. How do you prove value?**
Live KPIs from the deployment: raw events vs. incidents (noise suppressed), time from first touch to critical, techniques observed, containment time. We only show measured numbers.

**Q15. What would you build with another month?**
Real EDR/IdP/cloud telemetry connectors, Windows/RDP/AD/Kubernetes/cloud-storage decoys, adaptive decoys that mimic scanned targets, TAXII sharing, SOAR integration, and feedback-driven scoring.

### Demo-specific
**Q16. Is the attacker real?**
It's our own simulator inside an isolated lab, as the PS requires. Source IPs are Docker addresses mapped to RFC 5737 documentation addresses for display, and we say so openly.

**Q17. Why didn't you expose it to the real internet?**
Safety and reproducibility. A real deployment is possible with the controls in our safety doc, but unpredictable traffic would make a reliable demo impossible.
